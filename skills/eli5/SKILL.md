## The One Principle

**The pictures carry the explanation; the words only name what was just seen.** A reader who scrolls through this page looking at nothing but the drawings must still come away with the idea. If a sentence is doing the explaining and the drawing is illustrating the sentence, the drawing has failed and must be redrawn.

This is a sibling of `explain-diff`, `micro-world`, and `visual-doc`: they share design tokens, never structure. What separates this one is **the reader**, and the reader decides everything:

| Skill | Reader | Succeeds when |
|---|---|---|
| `explain-diff` | the developer who must merge it | they pass the comprehension quiz |
| `micro-world` | someone who learns by manipulating | they drive the scenario and feel the behavior |
| `visual-doc` | a domain-literate colleague | the findings are structured and navigable |
| **`eli5`** | **someone outside the domain entirely** | **they get it from the pictures alone** |

Because the reader is an outsider, the hard work is **subtraction**: choosing the few things that matter and drawing them, not compressing everything that exists. A complete-but-dense page is a failure here even though it would pass as a `visual-doc`.

Honesty rules apply throughout:

- Simplify by **omitting**, never by stating something false. "It works roughly like this" is fine; a wrong mechanism drawn confidently is not.
- Real numbers only, quoted from the source. Never invent a figure to make a bar chart look better.
- Do not flatten a genuine tradeoff into a happy ending. If the change costs latency or money, that beat stays in.

## Parse Arguments

| Argument | Meaning |
|----------|---------|
| (none) | Explain the subject already discussed in this conversation |
| a topic / question | Explain that subject ("how does DNS work") |
| a PR number / commit / branch | Explain what that change does and why, for an outsider |
| an issue number | Explain the problem the issue describes |
| a path (e.g. `src/auth/`) | Explain what that code does |
| `--code` | Lower the bar for evidence cards: a card may also quote the one call or condition a box stands for, even when the notes could state it |
| `--no-code` | No evidence cards at all; the notes carry every fact |

For a PR or issue, read it properly first: `gh pr view <n>`, `gh issue view <n>`, the diff, the files it touches. The page is only as good as what it is built from, and a PR body's own summary is usually written for insiders.

## Step 0: Applicability Gate

This skill needs **a mechanism worth drawing**: something moves, splits, fails, gets chosen between, or changes shape. That covers most architecture, algorithms, protocols, incidents, and design tradeoffs.

It has nothing to draw for a dependency bump, a rename, a formatting pass, or a config flip. Say so plainly ("이건 그림으로 얻는 게 거의 없어요") and offer `/explain-diff` instead. Do not manufacture filler pictures.

Also stop and hand off when **the reader is not actually an outsider**. If the person asking is the one shipping the change, they want `explain-diff`.

## Step 1: Find the One Idea and the Outsider

Before drawing anything, write down three things for yourself:

1. **The one idea.** A single sentence a non-expert could repeat afterward. Everything on the page serves it; anything that does not is cut.
2. **Who the outsider is.** A designer, a PM, an engineer from another team, a family member. This sets how much can be assumed and which analogies land.
3. **The confusion to dissolve.** The specific thing that makes this subject hard for that person.

If you cannot state the one idea in a sentence, you do not understand the subject well enough to simplify it yet. Go read more.

## Step 2: 그림의 순서를 먼저 정한다 (storyboard)

(이 절과 다음 절은 산출물의 문장을 정하는 자리라서 산출물 언어로 쓴다. 지시문을 영어로 읽고 한글로 옮기는 단계에서 번역투 제목이 생기기 때문이다.)

HTML을 쓰기 전에 페이지를 **그림의 순서**로 계획한다. 그림마다 제목 한 줄과, 그 그림이 보여줄 사실 한 줄(`제목 / 사실`). 사실 줄은 계획용이다: 페이지에서는 그림이 그 사실을 보여주고 마무리 줄이 한 번 말하며, 제목 아래에 따로 적지 않는다. 보통 5~8개 beat이고, 더 적어도 되며, 더 많으면 대개 두 페이지다.

**구성안은 처음부터 산출물 언어로, 최종 제목 그대로 쓴다.** 구성안의 제목이 그대로 페이지의 h2와 h3가 된다. 역할을 영어로 세운 뒤 한글로 옮기지 않는다: `how scores are read`를 옮기면 `점수 읽는 법`이 되고, `why the obvious fix breaks`를 옮기면 `왜 안 되는가`가 된다. 채팅에 구성안을 보여줄 때도 그 제목들이 아래 "제목은 그림이 무엇인지 말한다"의 기준을 통과해야 한다. 구성안에서 걸리면 그 자리에서 고친다.

기본 흐름. 대부분의 기술 주제에 맞는다. 굵은 글씨는 beat의 **역할**이고, `예:` 뒤는 그 역할에서 나온 **사실 줄**의 모양이다(제목은 그 앞에 붙는 짧은 대상 이름: `캐시 1차 시도` / `같은 쿼리는 호출 0회`). 역할 이름은 제목이 아니고, 사실 줄은 페이지에 글자로 들어가지 않는다: 그림이 보여주고 마무리 줄이 말한다.

1. **막히는 장면**: 실패하거나 막히는 모습부터 그린다. 해법으로 시작하지 않는다. 예: `쿼리 1개에 LLM 호출 4회, 응답 6초`
2. **처음 떠오르는 해법**: 합리적인 사람이 먼저 해 볼 방법. 예: `캐시 추가: 같은 쿼리는 호출 0회`
3. **그 해법이 깨지는 조건**: **이 beat가 페이지의 요점이다.** 원문에서는 표의 한 줄에 묻혀 있기 쉬우니 찾아낸다. 예: `쿼리 글자 하나만 달라도 캐시 미스, 적중률 3%`
4. **실제로 택한 방식**: 기계적인 동작으로 그린다. 예: `정규화 후 캐시: 공백과 대소문자 무시, 적중률 41%`
5. **대가**: 시간, 돈, 복잡도. 절대 빼지 않는다. 잰 값이 없는 주제(절차, 구조 설명)에서는 셀 수 있는 것(단계 수, 사람이 직접 하는 일의 횟수)을 그리고, 셀 것도 없으면 대가를 마지막 beat의 마무리 줄로 말한다. 값을 지어내 막대를 그리지 않는다. 예: `정규화 코드 120줄, 오탐 0.8%`
   주제가 실험이나 조건 비교(세팅 2개 이상 x 측정값 2개 이상, 또는 세팅 3개 이상)면 대가 바로 앞에 **결과 표** beat 하나를 둔다: 세팅 표(무엇을 어떻게 돌렸는지)와 결과 표(세트 x 세팅의 숫자). 그림이 하나의 비교를 보여주는 동안 표는 모든 조합을 한 자리에 둔다. 예: `실험 세팅 5가지와 세트별 결과` / `적중률은 dense 비교 질문에서만 오르고 공개 벤치마크는 그대로`. 아래 "Results table".
6. **기억할 한 문장**: 이 방식이 언제 적용되는지. 페이지에서는 맨 끝 `.close` 카드이고 위에 제목을 두지 않는다. 문장 자체가 제목이고, 제목과 카드 제목이 있으면 같은 말을 두 번 하게 된다. 머리 문장(`.t`)은 결정 하나를 말하는 한 문장, 45자 안, 쉼표 1개 이하다. 조건, 대안, 열거는 머리 문장에 쌓지 않고 아래 문단으로 내린다. 아래 문단(`p`)은 그 결정의 이유나 남은 확인 한두 문장이고, 진행 메모(캐시로 재는지, 프롬프트를 어디 싣는지, 첫 보고 항목이 무엇인지)는 여기 있을 자리가 없다: 해당 beat로 보내거나 지운다. 나쁜 예: `리랭커를 병합 뒤, 쿼리별 먼저, 합집합 전부에 각각 두고 종합 질문 hybrid에서 순이득 4문항을 넘는 위치가 있으면 그 경로에 리랭크 단을 붙이고, 없으면 v2 한 번 뒤 분해 순이익 없음으로 확정해요` (절 4개, 90자). 고친 예: 머리 `리랭커 위치 3곳 중 종합 질문에서 4문항 넘게 이기는 곳에 리랭크 단을 붙여요`, 문단 `세 위치는 병합 뒤, 쿼리별 먼저, 합집합 전부예요. 어디서도 4문항을 못 넘으면 v2를 한 번 더 돌리고 분해의 순이익이 없다고 확정해요.`

주제가 사고나 변경인데 독자가 그 시스템 자체를 모르면, 맨 앞에 "이것이 무엇을 하는가" beat 하나를 두고 그다음에 막히는 장면을 그린다(1 API가 하는 일, 2 500의 원인, …). 주제가 변경이 아니면 흐름을 바꾼다. "X는 어떻게 동작하나" 페이지는 들어오는 것, 거기에 일어나는 일, 나오는 것, 깨지는 곳 순서가 된다. 실패 beat는 있는 한 남긴다.

**beat가 6개 이상이면 묶음이 있는지 먼저 본다.** (5개는 기본 흐름 그대로면 평평하게 두고, 독자가 이름 붙일 묶음이 분명할 때만 묶는다.) 독자가 이름을 붙일 수 있는 두세 묶음(이 API가 하는 일, 500이 난 자리, PR 2건, 대가와 현황)으로 나뉘면 구성안을 두 단으로 쓴다: 묶음마다 사실을 말하는 제목 한 줄, 그 아래 beat 제목들. beat가 하나뿐인 묶음은 그 beat가 묶음이다. 묶음 제목도 beat 제목과 같은 기준을 통과해야 한다(`배경`, `해결`, `현황` 같은 역할 이름은 제목이 아니다). beat가 몇 개든 한 이야기로 이어지고 독자가 이름 붙일 묶음이 없으면 묶지 않는다: 묶을지는 beat 수가 아니라 묶음의 이름이 있는지가 정한다. 묶음은 페이지에서 상위 섹션(`1 / 4`)이 되고 beat는 그 안의 `1-1`이 된다 (Step 4 "Group the beats").

**그림 하나, 주장 하나.** 그림 하나에 제목이 둘 필요하면 그림이 둘이다.

**읽기 전에 알아야 하는 것은 앞에 있다.** 구성안의 beat마다 "이 그림을 읽기 전에 알아야 하는 것"을 한 줄 적고(없으면 `없음`), 그것이 앞 beat의 그림에 나오는지 확인한다. 없으면 앞에 beat를 두거나 이 beat의 그림에 그려 넣는다. 어느 줄이든 그 줄보다 위의 보이는 자리(그림, 해설)에 나온 것만 가리킨다. 위에 없는 것을 말해야 하면 그 줄에서 무엇인지 말하거나, 그것이 나오는 beat로 그 줄을 옮긴다. 접힌 블록 안의 정의는 정의가 아니다. 결과의 모양을 정하는 고정 조건(칸 수, 상한, 기준선)은 그 결과보다 먼저 그림에 나온다. 앞 beat의 해설, 접힌 블록, 마무리 줄은 뒤 beat에서 나올 것을 가리키지 않는다: 뒤에 나올 것의 이유가 여기 있더라도 그 말은 그것이 나오는 beat에서 한다. 채워 넣을 사실이 원문에 없으면 지어내지 않고 Step 6 보고서에 적는다.

### 제목은 그림이 무엇인지 말한다

위 흐름의 역할 이름(막히는 장면, 처음 떠오르는 해법, 깨지는 조건, 실제 방식, 대가, 한 문장)은 beat의 **역할**이지 제목이 아니다. 역할 이름 하나만으로 된 제목(`대가`, `원인`)은 탈락이고, 콜론 뒤에 사실이 따라오는 접두어(`대가: 제목 평균 11자에서 16자`, `500의 원인: temperature를 거부한 400`)는 된다: 접두어는 자리를, 뒤의 사실이 내용을 말한다. 제목, hero 제목, 행 캡션, 부제는 **주제에 대한 사실을 그대로** 쓴다. 개조식 환영.

- **hero 제목**: 주제의 실제 이름이 든 명사구. `vector_store 테이블 10개의 구조`, `파일 업로드가 청크가 되기까지`. `세 줄로 읽는 벡터 저장소`도, `보이지 않는 선반`도 아니다.
- **표지의 줄**: 읽는 사람이 가져갈 결과나 결론마다 `이름: 사실` 한 줄을 구성안에 적는다. 이름은 주제의 낱말, 사실은 개조식이다.
- **제목은 짧은 명사구 하나이고, 그 아래에는 아무 줄도 없다.** 제목(h2, h3)은 그림의 **대상**을 말하는 명사구로 20자 안팎(영문 식별자가 들어가면 조금 더), 한 줄에서 끝난다: `제목 생성 API의 동작`, `500의 원인`, `PR #2406: 프로필 헬퍼 공용화`, `대가와 현황`. 제목 다음은 바로 그림이다. 그림이 보여주는 **사실**(수치, 조건, 결과)은 그림 안의 막대와 라벨이 보여주고, 그 뜻은 그림 아래 마무리 줄이 한 번 말한다. 제목 아래 사실 줄(`.deck`)은 2026-11-10에 없앴다: 제목 아래 줄, 해설, 마무리 줄, 그림 라벨이 같은 숫자를 네 번 말하는 페이지가 나왔기 때문이다. 묶음(h2) 아래에도 줄이 없다. 읽는 사람은 제목으로 어디인지 알고 그림으로 무엇인지 안다. 긴 사실을 제목에 다 넣으면(`500의 원인: 새 US 기본 모델 gpt-6.1-sol이 temperature를 거부한 400`) 차례가 요약문이 되고 제목이 두 줄로 꺾인다. 예고(`함정`), 질문, 비유(`마법은 없다`)는 제목에 쓰지 않는다.
- **제목은 명사로 끝난다.** 서술어로 끝나는 문장은 제목이 아니라 해설이다. `갈래 4 하나가 근거 7건을 4~20위에 다 잡아, 병합 뒤 7건 전부 30위 안`은 제목 `하위 질문 4의 적중`으로 줄이고, 근거 7건의 순위는 그림이 그리며, "하나가 다 잡으면 병합 뒤에도 남는다"는 주장은 `그래서` 줄이 맡는다. hero 제목, 묶음 제목, beat 제목 모두 같다.
- **행 캡션과 부제**: 캡션은 그 행에서 참인 것을 주제의 말로 짧게 (`원본 테이블`, `캐시에 있음`), 부제는 데이터만 (`820ms`, `13자`, `astream_chat`, 상자가 든 값 그대로). `장부`, `본문과 벡터`, `값이 다름`, `1시간 안에 다시 올 때` 같은 설명과 조건은 그림 안에 쓰지 않고 해설로 내린다 ("Real names; a sub-line is data"). 비유는 페이지에 한 번, `.say` 줄 안에서, 글자 그대로의 말이 담지 못하는 뜻을 실을 때만 쓴다. 제목이 되지는 않는다.
- **번역투 금지.** `~의 여정`, `숨은 영웅`, `마법처럼`, `~를 만나다`, `한눈에 보는 ~`는 기계 번역의 표지다. 영어 용어를 한 글자짜리 한자어나 낯선 토박이말로 옮긴 이름도 같다: sub-query는 `갈래`가 아니라 `하위 질문`, candidate는 `후보`, chunk는 `청크`처럼 그 분야에서 실제로 부르는 말을 쓴다. 동료에게 소리 내어 말하면 어색한 구절은 지운다.
- **이식 검사**: 제목을 그림과 함께 읽어, 다른 주제의 페이지에 그대로 옮겨도 말이 되면 너무 추상적이다. `문제`, `원인 하나`는 탈락이고, `500의 원인`, `제목 생성 경로`는 통과다. 제목 하나만 보면 `대가와 현황`처럼 짧고 일반적일 수 있다: 주제의 이름은 hero와 그림이 채운다.
- **제목은 답이지 틀이 아니다.** 아래 패턴은 사실을 가리는 틀이다. 각각 가리고 있던 사실로 바꾼다.
  - 질문형 (`~하는가`, `~인가`, `~일까`, `왜 ~인가`): 답을 쓴다. `시험을 어떻게 내는가`는 `벤치마크 구성: 과제 묶음, 채점 규칙, 점수판`.
  - 분류 틀 명사 (`N축`, `~한 축`, `차원`, `관점`, `레이어`, `측면`): 축의 이름을 직접 쓴다. `평가의 4축`은 `채점 방식 3가지: 테스트, 루브릭, 사람 비교`.
  - 방법 명사화 (`~하는 법`, `~읽는 법`, `~하려면`): 대상과 값을 쓴다. `점수 읽는 법`은 `점수 세 종류: pass@1, 승률, 시간 지평`.
  - 단독 `A vs B`: 갈리는 값을 쓴다. `pass@1 vs pass^k`는 `pass@1은 1회 성공, pass^k는 k회 연속 성공`.
  - 강조 틀 (`핵심은`, `결국`, `본질적으로`, `진짜`, `단순히 ~가 아니다`, `~에 불과`): 지우고 사실만 남긴다.
  - 개수를 먼저 정하기 (`세 가지`를 먼저 세고 셋째를 채워 넣기): 항목이 먼저고 개수는 세어서 적는다.
  - 주제 전체를 감싸는 비유 (`모의고사`, `시험지`, `장부`): 제목과 구성안에 쓰지 않는다. `.say` 한 줄의 예외는 그대로다.
  - 본문에서는 `A vs B`나 `~하려면`이 평범한 문장일 수 있다. 이 규칙은 제목, 캡션, 구성안에 대한 것이다. Render check의 제목 grep은 그래서 제목 태그 안만 보고, 걸린 줄은 소리 내어 읽고 판단할 목록이지 실패가 아니다.

## Step 3: Draw the Mechanism

The drawings are hand-authored inline SVG, one per beat.

- **Depict the mechanism, not its name.** A box labeled "cache" says less than the prose. Draw the path a request takes, the two stores it sits between, the arrow that disappears when the cache is gone.
- **The cover test, before anything else.** Cover every piece of text in the drawing. If what is left says nothing (same-looking boxes joined by same-looking lines), you drew a labeled list, not a mechanism; redraw before you lay anything out. Boxes-and-lines alone is right only when the relationships *are* the content (a table structure, a dependency graph).
- **Draw the behavior with these shapes.** Most mechanisms are one of a few things, and each has a drawing:
  - *one becomes many*: one shape on the left, a stack of same-shaped cards on the right, a line from each slice of the source to its card, the cut marks drawn on the source
  - *repeat up to N*: a loop drawn back over the box it re-enters, with round markers `1 2 3 … N` on the loop
  - *the same thing comes back*: the same token shape (`A`) drawn where it is kept and where it arrives, so the eye matches them
  - *hit or miss, before or after*: two rows of the same path, one difference between them; what is skipped is drawn dimmed, not removed
  - *a choice*: the path forks; the branch not taken is drawn dashed and dim
  - *a failure*: the line leaves in red and stops at a small red X on the line (10px, stroke 1.6, as in `frame.html`; never heavier than that), and a dashed arrow carries on from the X to the unreachable box, which is dashed too. The stopped line is never left with a gap before that box
  - *cost or size*: lengths and counts drawn to scale, never a number alone. The shape is the bar pair: a 22px-tall `<rect class="dg-bar">` per value (gray for the reference state, `em` for the state that is the point, `bad` for the state that is worse), the value label in `.dg-l` right after the bar's end, the row name in `dg-l dg-row` at the left, one group per quantity (two groups side by side at most), each group under its `.dg-t` title and `.dg-rule` hairline ("A title inside a figure is bold" below). Numbers that are not drawn stay out of the figure ("A figure holds what it draws"). A bar is never narrower than 4px: when the true width would be less (4건 of 500건), draw 4px and let the label carry the exact value; the proportion is still honest because the other bar is to scale. A value of zero draws nothing: no bar, no dashed track the size of the maximum, no outline for the label to sit in. The label `0건` stands alone where the bar would begin (x = the bars' left edge + 8), and the empty row next to a full one is the picture. **A bar split into parts** (the shares of one whole: how 140 items fall into three outcomes, how a second splits into stages) is one 22px bar per row cut into segments 1px apart, one fill per part, chosen by what the part is and not by its size: `bad` for the part that is a failure, `em` for the part the beat is about, plain gray and `mid` (a darker gray) for the rest, in the same order in every row. Each value is written inside its segment (`<text class="dg-in">`, centered; `dg-in ink` on the plain gray fill). The part names are written once, in a `.legend` row inside the panel under the drawing (`<ul class="legend">`: one `<i class="sw">` and the name per part), and never beside the bar: a row that ends in `A 56%, B 35%, C 9%` says every name once per row. A value fits when its segment is at least the label estimate + 8 wide. The values that do not fit, a zero included, are the only text after the bar's end: `<text class="dg-out">` starting 6px past the bar, in segment order, 8px apart, each in its part's color (`dg-out dg-em`, `dg-out dg-bad`; a gray part's value stays plain) so the reader can tell which part it is. When two of the values written out are from gray parts, color cannot tell them apart, so each carries its part name (`놓침 0`, `무해 1`). An out-value keeps the unit the inside values have. A group title is the name of what was counted (`분해가 필요한 54문항`) and carries no verdict (`전부 켜는 것이 정답`): what the right answer is belongs in the note. Numbers that compare two states are always drawn this way; a table or a tile strip of numbers is not a figure
  - *what is absent or outside*: drawn as the missing thing's shape, never as a sentence in a box. A dashed rectangle whose only content is text (`골드 근거 7건 중 5건은 후보 30건 밖` inside a red dashed box) fails the cover test and never ships: draw the five missing cells past a dashed boundary, the hollow slot where the line should be, the dimmed box the path cannot reach
  - *which setting has which part* (the same parts, present or absent across settings, and nothing else differs): never a row of labeled boxes repeated for every setting with dashed boxes for what is missing. That writes each name once per row and draws no relation. It is a presence table: a `.fig.tab` panel holding `<table class="res ox">`, the parts as column heads once, one row per setting, and in each cell a drawn mark, `<span class="mk o" role="img" aria-label="있음"></span>` or `<span class="mk x" role="img" aria-label="없음"></span>` (drawn in the frame, not the characters `○` and `×`, whose sizes differ from font to font). Every cell is answered, so nothing is left for the reader to interpret; the cells the beat is about take `class="p"` on their present marks (one column, or the row of the setting being added). A part every setting has stays as a column only when it is the baseline the others are read against. The table has no caption unless the beat holds a second table, and then a title only. A cell whose answer is not yes or no holds the value (`3개`, `0.2`) or, for a third state, a word (`일부`). Boxes and arrows are for when the order or the flow is the point
  - *a ranked list of N slots* (a candidate list, a top-k): a strip of N small cells, a filled cell where a hit sits, and the rank written **under** the filled cell, never inside it: the cell is data, and a number on a filled cell reads as a badge. What lies outside the list (the items that did not make the top 30) is drawn outside the strip, as dashed cells past a dashed boundary, not as a text box: a box that only holds a sentence fails the cover test. When the strip is the result of merging k lists by quota, cut it into the quota blocks with dividers and name each block above it (`몫 7건`, `점수순 2건`), so the reader sees which block a hit landed in. Rows that compare lists (the original query, one sub-query, the merge) open with a caption pill and get no badges
  - *steps inside one system*: a system box (`<rect class="dg-sys">`, its name as a `.dg-t` line at its top-left inside (`dg-t code` when the name is an identifier such as `normalize_key()`), starting 16px in from the left edge when the box carries a badge on that corner so the two never touch) with the steps drawn as boxes inside it (`class="dg-box in"`: the panel fill, so they stand out from the white system box), the caller outside; the reader sees where the boundary is and which steps are the server's own. Edges from the caller to a step inside, and from a step back out, cross the system border by design; that crossing is not a defect. A system box that is the point of the figure takes `class="dg-sys em"` (brand stroke) rather than an inline style. A whole row that is history or the state replaced is wrapped in `<g class="dg-dim">`: solid strokes and labels at the secondary-text level (the frame sets .7 and keeps the sub-lines inside at the regular sub-line gray), because the reader has to read that row to compare it with the row that replaced it; a row faded to a watermark is a defect. Dashed is a different word on this page (absent, not taken, cannot reach), so a replaced or past state is never dashed
  - *a request crosses systems*: a sequence drawing. One head box per system in a row at the top, a dashed lifeline (`<line class="dg-life">`) down from each, and the messages as horizontal arrows top to bottom in time order, each labeled (`POST title/generate`, `400 거부`); a reply is dashed, the one that fails is red and stops at the small red X of *a failure* above (10px, stroke 1.6), and a label on every reply says what the status became in that hop (`502로 포장`). History that is not the point (an earlier 404) is drawn with `.dg-dim`. Use this whenever the claim is about where in a chain something happened; a row of boxes cannot show the return path
  The arithmetic rules below position these shapes; they do not replace them. A row of labeled boxes is the fallback, not the default.
- **Comparing options? Draw the difference.** Two states as two rows of the same path, with the one edge that changes between them. Separate labeled boxes with nothing connecting them is a restated list, not a comparison.
- **Label the arrows.** An unlabeled arrow means "related somehow". `writes`, `20장씩`, `19배 압축` is information. An edge label never carries a number that the beat says elsewhere: `켜짐 23/25` beside an arrow while the closing line says 23 is the same fact twice, and the label goes. When the row budget forces a short label, the label may shrink to the value alone (`0.2 포함`, `0.2 제거`) as long as the boxes on either side name the noun it refers to; a label that is only a verb (`전달`) or only a noun the boxes already show is noise and is dropped instead.
- **Concrete by default.** Draw the specific things, not categories of things: eight named tables in their three rows beats three ovals labeled 입력 / 처리 / 저장. If you catch yourself drawing "data flows to storage", name the data and name the storage. Abstract shapes are for subjects that have no artifacts (a concept, a policy), not a shortcut for subjects that do.
- **Real names; a sub-line is data, not explanation.** When the subject is a system, a box is the real thing under its real name: the table `vector_store_file`, the endpoint `POST /files`, the flag `use_doc_chunking`. Most boxes carry the name alone. A sub-line (`.dg-s` under the name) earns its place only when it is **data the drawing cannot show otherwise**: a number with its unit (`820ms`, `13자`, `항목 1개`), an identifier (`astream_chat`, `metadata.title`, class `dg-s code`), or the literal value the box holds (`"보험약관 암보험 보장 내역"`). A clause or a condition is explanation and never a sub-line: not `값이 다름`, `처음 온 문자열`, `1시간 안에 다시 올 때`, `나머지 전부 버림`, `제목 저장 자리`. Explanation has three homes, in this order: (1) the note under the figure for that badge, which is where the reader expects it; (2) the drawing itself, when the words were describing something the shapes can show (three keys that differ are three boxes named `키 1`, `키 2`, `키 3`; invisible spaces are drawn as `␣`); (3) an SVG `<title>` inside the box's `<g>`, shown on hover, for a qualifier the page can live without (a phone never shows it, so nothing the figure needs goes there). A sub-line never repeats across a row of same-kind boxes (`임베딩 호출 1회` under three boxes is one sentence in the note), and a pill or caption chip stands alone on its line: nothing follows it. File paths, line numbers and the mechanism in prose stay out of the drawing altogether.
- **Size by `viewBox`** (`viewBox="0 0 W H"`, CSS `width:100%; height:auto`), pick W and H for the content, and align shapes to a shared grid. Eyeballed offsets read as noise.
- **One stroke set, whatever the figure.** A flow of boxes, a strip of slots and a bar chart on one page must read as one family, so every shape takes its stroke from the classes in `frame.html` and none carries a `stroke`/`stroke-width` of its own: a box is `<rect class="dg-box">` (a hairline in `--dg-line` over a `--bg` fill), a connector is `class="dg-arrow"` with a marker whose polygon is `class="dg-head"`, a slot of a strip is `class="dg-cell"` (`on` and `off` for filled slots, which are parted by a panel-colored gap like a bar). Text inherits the page foreground (`currentColor`), so both themes work. The one element carrying the point of the picture adds `em` (brand) and the thing that breaks adds `bad` (red): `dg-box em`, `dg-arrow bad`; a colored arrow gets its own marker with `fill="var(--brand)"` or `fill="var(--red)"`. What is absent adds `absent` (dashed, one step darker, because a light dashed line disappears), and a dimmed shape uses `.dg-dim` (.7); nothing in a figure goes under .6. Emphasis is a color, never a heavier outline on a neutral box. A `.dg-s` sub-line under a `.dg-em` or `.dg-bad` name stays ink (the class sets `fill:currentColor`); only the name line takes the color. (`var(--blue)` still resolves, as an alias, and a shape written with `stroke="currentColor"` is re-toned by the frame, so older pages re-skin; new drawings write the classes.) Two accent colors per drawing at most.
- **Text stays at the two label sizes** (`.dg-l` 13px, `.dg-s` 11.5px in the body face; `.dg-s.code` 11px mono for identifiers) plus the bold `.dg-t` title and the optional `.dg-q` focal string below. A box holds its real name and **at most one sub-line**, never a sentence, and the sub-line passes the data test above: one fact (`statement_timeout 2000ms`) or one example (`"보험약관 암보험 보장 내역"`), never both, and never a list of fields from the source (`filters eq, tool_choice`, `failed_stores [vs_…]`): detail of that kind goes to the notes under the figure or to an evidence card. (A schema box in an ERD-style drawing is the exception: its lines are the columns.) Fit every line to its box with the arithmetic in "Fit the text before you draw" below.
- **A row name is a label, not a sub-line.** In a bar chart or a strip, the name of each row (`분해 없음`, `AS-IS`) is `class="dg-l dg-row"` (13px, secondary color), the same size as a box label in the flow next to it; `.dg-s` is for the sub-line under a name and for a caption under a strip segment. A chart whose row names are smaller than the box labels of the neighbouring figure reads as a different kind of picture.
- **A title inside a figure is bold, and a bar group's title carries a rule.** Some text in a figure names a part of the figure rather than a thing drawn in it: what a bar group measures (`LLM 호출 수`, `문항당 입력 토큰`), the name of a system box (`로컬 pipeline`). That text is `class="dg-t"` (13px, weight 700; `dg-t code` for an identifier), never `.dg-l` and never `.dg-s`. Written as `.dg-l` it has the size, weight and color of the value labels beside the bars, and written as `.dg-s` it is smaller and fainter than the rows it heads; either way the reader cannot tell that the rows below belong to it. A bar group's title sits alone on its line above the rows, and a hairline `<line class="dg-rule">` runs under it, 12px below the title's baseline, from the group's left edge (the x of its row names) to its right edge (the end of its widest value label; with two groups side by side, the end of each group's column, the two rules at least 40px apart). The first bar starts 16px below the rule. The rule shows how far the title reaches, so every group has its own and two groups never share one; a group stacked under another puts its title baseline 50px below the bottom edge of the last bar above it. A system box's name takes no rule: the box outline already shows what the name covers. A bar figure with one group whose measure the heading already names needs no title, and then has no rule either. Nothing else is bold apart from the optional `.dg-q` focal string and the `.dg-cap` state captions below: a box name, a row name, a value and an arrow label stay regular, because a figure with bold on every line has no title again.
- **One figure, one job.** A mechanism drawing (boxes, arrows, lanes) shows how; a bar drawing shows how much. The two never share an SVG: no `.dg-b` big number floating beside a flow, no bar under a row of boxes, no `적중률 3.1%` in the empty corner. The result a mechanism produces is said in that beat's closing line, and when two states are compared by a number it is drawn once, as a bar pair in the cost beat ("Numbers are drawn to scale"). A page therefore has no `.dg-b` at all; the shared class stays in the frame for the other skills.
- **State captions and the focal string, when rows compare states.** (optional; most figures use neither) A figure whose rows show the same thing in different states may give each row a header line above it: a pill `<rect class="dg-pill" height="20" rx="10">` (width = text width + 16) at the row's left edge with a `.dg-cap` label centered on it (baseline at pill y + 14), both carrying the same modifier, and nothing after the pill on that line: what the state concretely does is the first sentence of its note. The header line takes its own height in the layout plan, not a caption column. A row named by a pill gets no badge: the pill is its name, and its note under the figure opens with the same chip. When a before/after figure also backs a diff card (which needs a badge on the box of each state), do not use pills: name the rows with plain `.dg-s` captions in a left caption column (`AS-IS`, `TO-BE`) and badge the boxes; the card rule wins because the badge is what ties the card to the drawing (`<span class="cap em">` with the same text and modifier) instead of a bold name, so the reader finds the note by the chip. A pill is small on purpose (20px, 11.5px text); a pill the size of a button reads as a badge. Pick the caption by what the rows compare; these pairs are recommendations, not a fixed list. One change that has already happened reads well as `BEFORE` / `AFTER`; one change that is proposed or planned, as `AS-IS` / `TO-BE`. Rows that are not one change over time (two cases, two modes, two inputs) read well when each is named by what is true in that row, a short noun phrase in the subject's terms. Use `.bad` for the state that breaks, `.em` for the state carrying the point, and no modifier for a state that is neither: a red caption claims the state is wrong. When the point of the figure is the text itself (a search query, a command, an error message), one string may add `.dg-q` to its `.dg-l` (14px, weight 600; `.dg-em`/`.dg-bad` still color it): the one the figure is about, once per figure. A row of variants of a string (three spellings of the same query) stays `.dg-l` in quotes; three bold strings in a figure read as a heavier typeface, not as emphasis. Every other label stays at the regular sizes, and the pills count toward the two accent colors.
- **Every figure gets** `role="img"` and an `aria-label` stating the same claim as its heading. (An interactive figure points `aria-labelledby` at its `그래서` line instead, because the claim changes with the input.)
- No `<script>`, `<style>`, or `<foreignObject>` inside the SVG (the script of an interactive figure sits at the end of `<body>`; see "An interactive figure" below). No emoji, no icon fonts, no external resources. Long decorative path data means the drawing is too elaborate: simplify it.

### Fit the text before you draw (no renderer needed)

SVG text does not wrap and does not push boxes apart. Every clipped label and every pair of overlapping labels in a shipped page came from a label that was wider than the room drawn for it. So **size the room from the label, never the label from the room**. Plan with the per-character widths below, then measure: when a browser exists, the same `measure.html` run reports `data-fit`, every label that leaves its viewBox, crosses a box edge, or overlaps another label in the rendered font (number badges excluded), and the page ships only when it prints `none`. The measurement wins over the table both ways: a label the table says fits but the render says crosses is a defect, and a gap the table made wider than the measured text may be tightened. The widths are Chrome measurements rounded up, deliberately generous for the system fonts the page will be read in (Apple SD Gothic Neo, Pretendard, Segoe UI); a line that passes them fits, and with no browser they are the only check.

| Text | Width per character |
|---|---|
| Hangul, in any class | 1.0 × font-size (`.dg-l` 13px, `.dg-s` 11.5px, `.dg-s.code` 11px) |
| `m` `w` `M` `W` in `.dg-l` | 1.0 × font-size (13px) |
| other Latin lowercase, digits, `_` `-` `/` `.` in `.dg-l` | 0.6 × font-size (8px) |
| Latin uppercase in `.dg-l` | 0.75 × font-size (10px) |
| space, `(`, `)`, `:` in `.dg-l` | 5px |
| every non-Hangul character in `.dg-s.code` (mono), spaces included | 7px |
| non-Hangul in `.dg-s` (body face, 11.5px) | digits and lowercase 6.5px, uppercase 7.5px, space and punctuation 3.5px |
| anything not listed (`×`, `→`, `=`, `+`, `…`) | 1.0 × font-size |
| `.dg-t` (figure title, 13px bold) | Hangul 13px, `m` `w` and Latin uppercase 11px, other Latin lowercase and digits 8.5px, space and punctuation 5px; `dg-t code` (mono 12px) 7.5px for every non-Hangul character |
| `.dg-q` (optional focal string, 14px, weight 600) | Hangul 14px, `m` `w` and Latin uppercase 13px, every other non-Hangul character 9px, space 5px |
| `.dg-cap` (optional state caption, 11.5px bold, body face) | Hangul 11.5px, digits and lowercase 7px, uppercase 8px, space and punctuation 3.5px; its pill is the text + 16 wide (8px each side, the same padding as the `.cap` chip under the figure). The old row (Hangul 13, Latin 10) belongs to the 12px caption of visual-doc and explain-diff and makes an eli5 pill 20 to 30px too wide; the Render check measures the pill against the rendered text |

Line height: 18px per `.dg-l` line, 15px per `.dg-s` line, 19px per `.dg-q` line. A `<tspan>`'s `dy` is the height of the line **above** it (a `.dg-s` sub-line right under the `.dg-l` name has `dy="18"`; the next `.dg-s` line has `dy="15"`), so the box-height formula below and the `dy` values agree.

1. **Box from label.** Take the widest line of the box, estimate it, add 28 (14px padding each side), round up to a multiple of 10: that is the box width. Height is 14 + (sum of line heights) + 14; that is a minimum, and boxes in one row share the tallest height. First-line baseline: `y = box top + 25` (14 padding + the 13px name's ascent), or `+ 27` when the first line is a 15px `.dg-q`; each later line moves by its `dy` (a `.dg-s` line under a `.dg-q` line has `dy="20"`). In a box taller than the minimum, center the block instead: `y = box center − (Σ dy)/2 + 4`. A box is never narrower than its text; if the result is wider than you want, shorten the text.
2. **Lines grow the box, never the other way.** A box holds a name line and at most one sub-line ("Text stays at the two label sizes" above); the one exception is a schema box in an ERD-style drawing, whose lines are its columns and which holds as many `<tspan x="{box center}" dy="…">` lines as it has columns, the height formula absorbing them. Do not cut a column list to make a schema box small. A box that wants a third line elsewhere is two boxes, or its detail moves to the notes.
3. **Row budget.** For every row: (caption column + 40, when rows carry a left-hand caption; size that column from its widest caption line like any other label) + Σ box widths + Σ gaps ≤ viewBox width. **The panel supplies the inset and the drawing supplies none**: every panel pads 16px above and below and 20px at the sides, so the viewBox is `0 0 W H` with the first shape or label at x=1, the last one ending at W-1, the top shape at y=1 and the bottom one at H-1. The 1px keeps a stroke from being clipped and keeps a label's edge from measuring as outside the viewBox. Text needs its height above the baseline: the topmost label has its baseline at y=14 or lower. A side where a badge hangs on a corner keeps 10px (the exemplars start their boxes at x=10, y=10 for that reason). An empty band inside the viewBox adds to the panel's inset, and the figure then sits deeper than the table and the text card next to it. Gaps are at least 40; a gap that carries an arrow label is at least the label estimate + 24. When a row does not fit, shorten labels first, then break it into two rows, then (last) widen the viewBox up to 840. Never above 840: the figure is about 780px wide on screen, so a wider viewBox shrinks 13px text below 12px. Height is unlimited: more boxes mean more rows, not smaller text.
4. **Nothing crosses a border.** Every box label is `text-anchor="middle"` at the box center, and rule 1 guarantees it stays inside. Free-standing text (edge labels, row captions) gets a clear zone: no other text, no line other than the one it labels, and no box edge within 6px of its estimated extent, and that extent stays inside `[1, W-1]`. Vertically, a line of text occupies baseline −12 to +3 for `.dg-l` and `.dg-t`, −10 to +3 for `.dg-s`, −13 to +4 for `.dg-q`; use those for the clear zone, not just the horizontal estimate.
5. **Edges never pass through a box.** A line that crosses a box it does not connect is a defect, whichever direction it runs. When an edge has to skip a row, route it down a lane between boxes and turn below that row. Two edges that end on the same box land at least 20px apart along that side (or merge into one elbow before arriving). Give each `<svg>` its own marker id (`ar1`, `ar2`, …): a page has several drawings and duplicate ids are invalid.
   Preferred, not required: horizontal and vertical segments with an elbow (`M x1 y1 V ymid H x2 V y2`) rather than a diagonal, and the label on a straight segment: baseline 10px above a horizontal segment with `text-anchor="middle"`, or beside a vertical one with `text-anchor="end"` at `x = line x − 8`, in class `.dg-s`. If the two endpoints differ by less than 12px, draw the edge straight. A diagonal is fine when it is the clearer line; then its label carries `dg-halo` so it stays readable where it crosses. Rows stacked above each other are at least 40px apart (box bottom to next box top) when an edge label sits between them, 30px otherwise.
6. **Text comes last.** Inside each `<svg>`, write every `<rect>`, `<line>`, and `<path>` first and every `<text>` after them. SVG paints in document order, so a line written after a label is drawn over it, and `dg-halo` only works when the text is painted on top.
7. **Plan first, as a comment.** The first child of every `<svg>` is a one-line layout plan, and writing it is the check:
   `<!-- W=760 | row1 y=10 h=88: [10..160] gap45 [205..355] gap45 [400..550] gap50 [600..750] -->`
   Every range is closed, adjacent ranges differ by exactly the gap, the last end is ≤ W-1. If the arithmetic does not close, the drawing does not ship.
8. **No ceiling on boxes.** A subject with twelve tables gets twelve boxes in one drawing if that is what the reader needs to see at once; the row budget and the 840px width are the only limits, and height grows with rows. Split into two drawings only when the subject itself has two stories (a current path and an old path), each with its own `.say`.

### An interactive figure, only when a value has to move

Every figure is static unless the subject passes this test (the same test as in `visual-doc` and `explain-diff`):

- **Static first.** Two fixed states (before/after, on/off, hit/miss) are drawn as two rows of one static figure: the eye compares both at once, and a toggle would hide one while showing the other.
- **Interactive only when the reader has to move a value to see the point.** That means the outcome changes across three or more values of one input (a count, a size, a rate, a threshold), or the point is the value at which the behavior flips (`요청 1개면 거부 없음, 2개부터 거부`). Then one or two inputs redraw the figure.
- **Three questions before building.** What is the input? What moves in the drawing when it changes? What does the reader learn at a value other than the default? If the third has no answer, draw it static.
- **The default state already makes the point.** A skimmer, a screenshot, and a printed page see only the state at load.
- **Never a step-through** that reveals already-visible content in order, and never an input that only shows or hides text.

On an eli5 page, additionally:

- At most one interactive figure per page. It replaces the static figure of its beat; it is not an extra picture.
- Controls are the `.ctl` row from `frame.html` (a range with its `<output>`, or a `.seg` switch), placed above the `.fig`. One or two inputs. No free-text input, no play button.
- The script is one `<script>` at the end of `<body>` that redraws a `<g>` inside the figure; nothing goes inside the `<svg>` element itself. It computes the outcome with the subject's real rule (the honesty rule covers it: no invented curve) and builds shapes first, labels last.
- The drawing rules do not relax. Do the fit arithmetic for the smallest and the largest input; the layout-plan comment describes the widest state.
- The `그래서` line under the figure is the readout: the script rewrites that one `.say` (keep its badge, `aria-live="polite"`, two sentences at most) to say what happens at the current value, and `aria-labelledby` on the figure points at it. The notes and badges stay static; they describe the parts, not the current value. The default value is the one that shows the page's point.
- A subject that needs several steps in order, a mission, or state that carries over between actions is a `micro-world`, not a figure. Say so and hand off.

### The word budget

- **The numbers in this section are defaults, not quotas.** They describe a page that reads well for a subject of ordinary size: a note of about 80 characters, a closing line of about 60, a fold of about three sentences, one or two tables. A subject with more parts gets more beats, a figure with more to say gets a longer note or a fold, a result measured in two units gets a table for each. What stays fixed is the order of preference when something does not fit: draw it, then say it in a note, then fold it. Never pad a line to reach a number, and never cut a fact the reader needs in order to make one. When a line or a count runs well past its default, say why in the Step 6 report.
- Under every figure come the **notes**: a `.notes` list with one `.note` per numbered badge in the drawing, each `badge + the thing's name in bold + one short sentence, two at most` saying what that part is and the one condition the drawing could not show. **A note runs to about 80 characters** (Korean syllables, spaces included). Most are shorter; one that carries a condition the drawing cannot show may run to 110 or so. Past that the note is explaining, which is the drawing's job: ask whether the extra belongs in the figure, in a second note, or in the fold. Then **one closing `.say` line**: one sentence of about 60 characters not counting its badge, up to 90 or so when the sentence needs its condition (a second clause only when it is the condition of the first), which says the one thing the reader takes from this figure and does not read the figure back; after a notes list it opens with a badge (see "Two badges" below), so it cannot be read as one more note. **One number, one place.** A number the figure shows (a bar label, an edge label, a table cell) is not written again in that beat's notes or `.say`, and stands in a heading only when the number is the name of what the section is about (`500의 원인`); the prose says what the number means, not the number. A number the figure does not show may appear once, in the note or the `.say`, never in both. When something stayed unverified, one more `.say` line labeled `확인 못 한 것` (`lab warn`) goes before it, and `[추정]` never hides inside a sentence. Facts only, in the subject's own words, body size. Do not repeat the heading, do not repeat what a label in the picture already says, do not add background.
- **A note opens with a mark the reader can find in the figure** (or in the evidence card it is about). Pick the mark from what the figure already uses to tell its parts apart, and use one kind for the note heads of a figure: a numbered badge (`.m`, the same number as the `dg-m` on the box); the row chip (`.cap`) when rows are named by caption pills; a color key when the figure tells the parts apart by fill: the same swatch the figure's `.legend` uses (`<i class="sw"></i>`, `sw em`, `sw bad`), written inline before the bold name in a `.notes.plain` note (`<div class="note"><div><i class="sw em"></i><b>{name}</b>…</div></div>`), so the swatch sits on the text line and not in a column of its own; or **the same words in bold and nothing else** when the figure prints that name itself (a bar row `분해 없음`, a chart title). A number that is part of such a name (`원문만 50건`) stands in the note head with it; "One number, one place" is about measured values, not names. When the figure already prints the name, add no mark: a badge hung in the margin beside a row name, or a bracket tying two rows to one number, is a mark the layout has no room for, and it is left out. A note about two named parts writes both names (`<b>A</b>와 <b>B</b>는`) instead of a shortened pair the figure does not show. A note that opens with bold words the figure does not contain, with no badge, chip or key, cannot be traced to the picture: rename it to what the figure shows or give it a mark.
- **Two badges, and the lime one is rare.** The lime `.lab` is the page's one signature mark, so it marks only what the page is for: **the closing lines that the `정리` headline restates**. Write the `.close` headline first, then find the one or two closing lines it rests on (usually one, since the headline is one sentence; one per clause at the most); those get the lime badge, and its word is `그래서` unless the line is a decision or a criterion that reads wrong after `그래서` (`선택`, `확정 기준`). Every other closing line that follows a notes list gets the **outline badge** (`<span class="lab q">`), which only separates the line from the notes above it and says what the line is. **The outline badge has no word list.** Its text is what this line is in relation to this figure, in the subject's own words, one to three words: a condition all rows share, the scope of the measurement, an exception, what came before, the present state (`공통 조건`, `이전 실험`, `머지 뒤`, `리뷰 상태`: examples from three different subjects, not a list to pick from). The test is the one for headings: if the same badge could sit on another beat's closing line and still make sense (`참고`, `정리`, `포인트`, `요약`), it is too general. One wording appears at most twice on a page, `그래서` included. A beat carries one closing line and one badge; a line that holds two things (a cost and a criterion) is two lines only when both matter, and otherwise the lesser one goes. `확인 못 한 것` (`lab warn`) is a third kind, text-only, outside these counts. A closing line that stands alone under a figure with no notes needs no badge. On a page of nine beats, seven lime badges is the defect this rule exists for: the highlight then marks nothing.
- **The cover says what the reader takes away, not how it was found.** Fact rows (`.facts`) under the title, one per result or conclusion: a short name from the subject and one fact with its measured number, 개조식 (a row is a table cell, not running text). A row reads without the body, and it does so through its name: the name is in plain words, never a term the page explains later, and nothing is added to explain it (no sentence before the value, no small line under the name, no note under the rows). **A number is shown, not narrated.** The value side holds the values and nothing around them: `56.4% → 81.4%`, not `140문항 중 적절한 복원. 56.4%에서 81.4%`; `6.0초 → 1.2초`, not `6.0초에서 1.2초로 줄었어요`. A value that did not change is written once, without `그대로`. **Pick the simplest shape the rows fit** (all in `frame.html`): a row with one value or one before and after pair is a name and a `.v`; a row that has a value for each of two or three conditions lists them in a line (`.pairs`: condition name, value); when two or more rows run over the same conditions and every cell fills, they become the cover grid (`table.fg`), with the conditions in the head once and, in the corner cell, what a pair is (`v5 → v7`) or what every row is out of (`400명 중`). The grid is for when it falls out of the data: rows that do not share conditions, or a grid that would leave cells empty or exist for one row, stay as rows. With more than four conditions the cover carries only the ones the conclusion rests on, and the full grid is the body's results table. **A conclusion row is a verdict**: `rerank를 제품에 반영하지 않음`, `gpt-5.4 충족, solar-pro4 미충족`. Its reasons and conditions are in the body and the close, not in the row, and that holds for the number a decision rests on too. A decision still open is a row with the recommended option (`둘 다 측정 권장`); a status is a row with where things stand (`스모크 완료, 본 측정 보류`). In a cover grid a verdict that differs by condition goes in the condition cells (`충족`, `미충족`), and spans the columns (`td.txt`) only when it is one statement about all of them. The body uses the cover's row names: when the source's own name for a measure is a term, the plain name replaces it in both places. Usually two to four rows; a single takeaway is one `.lede` sentence. With no result yet, what the page sets out to settle. Method and motive go in the sections below.
- A figure whose rows are named by caption pills keeps `.notes.plain` with one note per row, each opening with the row's chip (`.cap`, same text and modifier as the drawing's pill), in row order. A figure with no badges and no pills (a number strip, a single-row picture) keeps a plain `.say` of two or three sentences instead of notes, with no badge (there is no notes list for it to be confused with); nothing goes inside the card (no caption).
- **Numbered badges tie the three places together, and the numbers live in the drawing.** A badge is `<g class="dg-m">` on the top-left corner of a box in the drawing, `<span class="m">` in the head of the evidence card that backs that box, and at the head of the matching `.note`. The drawing is the only place that hands out numbers: a card never gets a number of its own, it repeats the badge of the box it is evidence for, so a beat with two badges has two numbers everywhere, however many cards it carries. Number in reading order of the drawing (top to bottom, then left to right within a row; a box that spans rows takes the order of its top edge), restart at 1 in every beat, and never badge what has no note.
- **The number says where, the look says what follows, and a badge carries no color.** A badge is one of two things, and both are the same 18x18 square so they line up in the drawing, the card head and the notes. The note badge is a gray square: `<rect x="-9" y="-9" width="18" height="18" rx="4"/>` with the digit at `y="4"` in SVG, `class="m"` in HTML; it means a note under the figure and nothing else. The terminal badge (`code`) means an evidence card with that number sits under the figure: the same square dark (`<rect x="-9" y="-9" width="18" height="18" rx="4"/>`), a title strip (`<rect class="bar" x="-9" y="-9" width="18" height="4.5"/>`), three dots (`<circle class="d1" cx="-6.6" cy="-6.8" r="1"/>`, `d2` at `cx="-3.8"`, `d3` at `cx="-1"`) and the white digit at `y="6"`; in HTML `class="m code"`, which draws the strip and dots itself. Its colors are fixed in both themes, so it never looks like the gray square. Neither badge takes a meaning color: red for what breaks belongs to the drawing (a red cell, a red mark on the line, a dashed red box), and a red badge only makes the reader ask why the number is red. Never let a badge look like a piece of the data: a filled blue circle next to filled blue cells is two things in one costume. The same badge has the same look in all three places, so the reader knows from the drawing which parts have code to look at.
- **One sequence per figure.** Number the badges in the reading order of the drawing whatever their kind: `1` note, `2` terminal, `3` note. Never run two sequences (code 1, 2 and note 1): the number's job is to be found, and two `1`s in one drawing stop the eye. A card repeats its box's number, so two cards backing one box carry the same number twice.
- **A badge earns its place; it is not an index.** Badge a box only when the note says something the box label cannot (a limit, an error code, a condition) or when the name alone could not point the reader at it. When every box in a drawing carries a badge, the numbers stop highlighting and start counting; merge the notes that describe one action (an LLM call and its output are one step, not two) until some boxes stay unbadged. Three badges in a beat is the usual ceiling; a beat that wants more is two beats. A drawing that is itself a list (three decision rows one under the other, each with its own title) gets no badges at all: the order is the number, and its notes open with the row's title in bold (`.notes.plain`, no `.m`), in the same order as the rows. A drawing whose rows open with caption pills is the same case: the pill is the name, the notes open with the same chip (`.cap`), and badging every row would turn the badges into a count.
- **A badge never covers a label.** The badge sits on the top-left corner of a box, so the box's own text, which is centered, stays clear of it. A badge belongs only to a shape that has a corner to sit on: a bar row, a strip, a free-standing label have none, so they get no badge. A bar chart's rows are named by their `dg-l dg-row` row labels and its notes are `.notes.plain`, each opening with the row name in bold. Before shipping, check every `dg-m` transform against the text positions of its figure: a badge whose 18x18 square intersects the extent of any `<text>` (rule 4's clear zone) is moved or removed.
- **One note for several boxes means one group badge.** When a note is about several same-shaped boxes at once (every store in a fan-out, the three results that are noise), never badge the first box and leave the rest bare; the reader takes the badge to mean that one box. Draw a dashed group outline (`<rect class="dg-grp">`, 8px outside the boxes on every side, `rx` 10, `bad` for a red group) before the boxes, draw the boxes after it so they sit on top of it, and put the one badge on the group's top-left corner. The note then names the group with its count (`store 5개`, `무관 결과 3묶음`). Arrows may cross the group outline; the badge never does.
- Prose chips: a `<code>` chip in a note names a thing the reader can see in the drawing or in an evidence card of that beat; a chip that appears nowhere else on the page is noise and becomes plain words.
- **One name per thing, the actor as subject, the count as a number.** (shared across eli5, explain-diff, micro-world, visual-doc) Pick one name for each thing and each action and keep it everywhere on the page, labels and drawings included: once it is `액세스 토큰` it is never `인증 토큰`, `세션 키`, or `자격 증명`; once it is `재발급` it is never `갱신`. A second name reads as a second thing. When two different things share a word, qualify both (`API 서버`, `인증 서버`, never a bare `서버`). Make the subject the thing that acts: `인증 서버가 두 번째 요청을 거부했어요`, not `두 번째 요청이 거부됐어요`; a sentence that hides the actor hides where the cause is. Write the count the source gives (`요청 3개`, `3번`), not `여러 개` or `요청 수만큼`. A coined name, one made up to tell things apart (a setting code, a case or dataset id, an earlier round), appears only below the place where the page says what it is: the title and every heading call the thing by what it does. In each later section its first use carries what it does beside it. Something the page never introduces is called by what it is, never by its name. A name the reader could look up in the subject's code is not coined.
- **A particle is part of the word before it; a count stays on one line.** (shared across eli5, explain-diff, micro-world, visual-doc) Attach every particle and ending to the word before it, also after an English word, a number, or a code chip: `Job은`, `store에도`, `API와`, `400이`, `<code>job_indexing.mode</code>가`, `automatic인`. Never `Job 은` or `</code> 을`: where `word-break: keep-all` applies a line breaks only at a space, so that space lets the particle start a line. Choose the particle by how the word is read aloud (`Job은`, `API는`, `store를`). Write a digit and its unit together (`3개`, `0행`, `1회`), and put `&nbsp;` between a native numeral and its unit (`한&nbsp;번`, `두&nbsp;줄`, `세&nbsp;가지`) so the line never breaks inside the count. Bind nothing else with `&nbsp;`: each one makes a longer unbreakable run and a more ragged right edge. Before finishing, run `grep -nP '[A-Za-z0-9_)]+(</code>)? (은|는|이|가|을|를|의|에|와|과|로|으로|도|만|에서|에는|에도|인|인데)(?=[ ,.<)]|$)'` on the file and fix every hit that is a particle (`이` and `가` can also be a word of their own), except inside source quoted verbatim in a code block or an evidence card, which keeps its spacing.
- **When a beat needs more than the caps allow, the extra goes into one folded block, not into longer lines.** A `<details class="more">` after the notes (before the `.say`) holds the condition, exception or reason the drawing and the notes could not carry: about three sentences, 해요체, closed by default, with a `summary` that names what is inside (`예외 2건`, `2차 호출이 기준인 이유`), never `자세히` alone. It repeats no number the figure shows and never carries the beat's conclusion (that is the `.say`). Usually fewer than half the beats carry one; when every beat wants one, or one beat wants three paragraphs, ask whether the page is a `visual-doc`. The default page has none: the fold exists so that the visible text can stay short without the page becoming false by omission.
- No paragraphs of body prose anywhere on the page outside that fold.
- There is no `figcaption`. A fact that used to go in a caption goes in the `.say` if the reader needs it to read the picture, and is dropped otherwise.
- **File paths and line numbers stay off the page.** An outsider will never open `app/models/store.py:103`. The names of the things drawn (a table, an endpoint, a flag) are not evidence of that kind; they are the nouns of the subject and belong in the drawing (see "Real names; a sub-line is data" above). A name the drawing does not show may still appear once, in that beat's notes, when the reader will actually meet it. The one place a file name is allowed is the head of an evidence card (`config/config.onprem.yaml`), because there the file itself is the thing shown; line numbers stay off even there.
- **No footer, no source list.** File paths, line numbers, and the list of what you read are evidence for the person who asked, not content for the reader. They go in your report (Step 6), never in the HTML.
- **Provenance is one line in the hero eyebrow**: the source and a date, e.g. `studio-pipeline, 2026-09-28`. Nothing else goes there: no commit hash, no "기준", no status word. If there is no meaningful source (a general "how does DNS work"), the eyebrow is just the date.
- **Numbers are drawn to scale.** The cost beat is a figure like every other beat: before/after bar pairs (`.dg-bar`, see "Draw the behavior with these shapes"), two quantities at most, and at least one of them is a cost; the numbers that are not drawn do not ride along in the figure (next item), and the `.say` under it says what the numbers mean. There is no tile strip and no `.numbers` block, and no table of two values; a grid of conditions and measures is a results table (next item), which comes before this beat. A number already stated in an earlier beat's closing line is drawn here and may stand in this section's heading (a heading is the title, not prose), but is not written again in this beat's notes or `.say`. Elsewhere on the page, two states compared by a size are also drawn to scale when the shape allows it: two output boxes whose widths are proportional to their text lengths count as drawn to scale.
- **A figure holds what it draws and its names, nothing else.** This covers every block that shows values or states: a drawing, a results table, a strip. Inside it go the things drawn (boxes, arrows, states, bars) with their names, and, when there is one, the name of the axis or the measure (a chart's title line, `.dg-t`). What does not go inside: a small line at the bottom listing conditions, sources or numbers that were not drawn; a tag beside a row chip or a table caption that explains the row, repeats the heading, or says how to read the marks (bold, underline, color). A box sub-line is still governed by "Real names; a sub-line is data". For each such line ask one question: **if this line were deleted, would the reader misread the figure?** A condition that passes (which population the bars are over, the unit, the baseline) goes into one folded `<details class="res-more">` at the bottom of the panel, with a `summary` that names what it holds (`측정 조건`), the same block a table uses. A measured number that fails the question leaves the page and is listed in the Step 6 report. A fact that is not a measurement and appears nowhere else (the present status, a decision: `사람 리뷰 0건`) moves to the closing line instead of being dropped. Most figures end up with no fold at all: a fold on every figure is the same clutter one click away. If a tag beside a chip is a name the notes use (`단발 경로`), it is part of the chip text or the note says it, not a tag.
- **Results table.** When the subject has results over conditions (two or more settings and two or more measures, or three or more settings on one measure: an experiment, a benchmark, a comparison of configurations), the reader needs every combination in one place, and a bar pair cannot hold it. The page then has a results-table beat right before the cost beat: a `.fig.tab` panel per table, usually one or two tables (`.res`, see `frame.html`). **When there is no table**: one measure over two settings is the cost beat's bar pair, not a table; numbers quoted here and there in a report are not a grid until the same measure exists for several settings; a page about a mechanism, an incident or a design has no results table at all, and a page without a grid passing Render check 7 with zero tables is the normal case. The table is a response to the shape of the source, never a section every page gets. **Table 1, the settings, only when the settings differ in two or more things** the results table's column headers cannot carry (how the split is decided and how many pieces; a model and a prompt); when the settings differ in one thing, the column headers are the settings and there is no settings table. Table 1 has one row per setting as it was run, a short phrase or a number per cell (`항상`, `2~4`, `1회`), one column per thing that differs between settings and nothing that is the same for all of them (a condition every setting shares was drawn in an earlier beat; if it was not and the table is misread without it, it goes in the folded `.res-more`, never beside the caption); no result in this table. **Table 2, the results**: rows are the sets or conditions, columns are the settings (or the measures), a cell is one number and nothing else. **Only measured settings get a column**: a setting (or a set) with no value anywhere in the table is not a column (row) of the results table; the settings table is where every setting is listed, and an all-blank column says nothing but costs width. **Columns are typed**: a text column is left-aligned, a number column (`class="n"` on its `th` and every `td`) is right-aligned at equal width, and the header follows its column, so a header never sits on the other side from its cells. Nothing rides beside a number: n lives in the row label (`비교 질문 55, dense`), 승패 and deltas go to the folded notes or stay in the drawing, a row that has only a delta and no value is left out of the table. The `.res-cap` above each table is two lines: the number line (`<span class="res-no">표 1</span>`, set like a section's number line) and the title (`<span class="res-t">세팅</span>`). A page with one table has no number line: a number says there is another. The caption holds nothing else: no unit, no population, no direction, no shared condition and no legend for bold and underline (the marks are a convention, and a page does not explain its own form). A unit is always visible, in the cells (`56.4%`, `18건`) or in the column header (`평균 초`), never only in the fold; what a ratio is over, and that lower is better when it is, go in the folded `.res-more`. **A header with two levels** (`.res.h2`): when the columns fall into groups, each group name is a `th.grp` centered over its own columns with a rule only as wide as the group (`<th class="grp" colspan="2"><span><i>1단계</i>{group name}</span></th>`; the `<i>` holds the step or group number when the source numbers them, and is left out otherwise), the first column of each group takes `g0` on its `th` and on every `td` so a gap parts the groups, a group of one column is a `th.grp` with no `colspan`, and a column outside any group spans both header rows. The row side is the same: a condition made of two things (a model and a prompt, a dataset and a retriever) is two columns, the outer one written once (`<td class="outer" rowspan="N">`, with `tr.gs` on the first row of each group) and the inner one in `td.inner`; a condition of one thing stays one column, and a condition of three is two columns with two of the three joined in the outer one. With grouped rows or grouped columns the marks compare inside each group, never across groups. A group header the same size and color as the columns under it, pushed to one side, does not read as a level. **Marks are mechanical**: `<b>` on the best value of each row (or of each column when the table compares down a column; one table uses one direction), `<u>` on the second when the row compares three or more values, a tie shares the mark, a row with one value gets none. The marks follow the number, never the author's view: no adjective, no arrow, no color in a cell. **A cell the source does not give stays empty** and nothing is estimated to fill it. **Nothing visible sits under the table but the table**: blanks, 승패, and how a derived value (a base plus a stated delta) was made go in one `<details class="res-more">` whose summary names what it holds (`주석: 빈칸, 승패, 계산`), folded by default; a reader who wants them opens it, the others see a clean grid. A per-cell fact may also ride as a `title` attribute for hover. The beat's notes are `.notes.plain`, one line per table saying how to read it, not restating cells; its `.say` states the one fact the table shows. One or two tables is the usual page. A further results table is right when a set is measured in a different unit and cannot share a grid (it gets its own caption and unit), or when the reader needs a second measure next to the first; a page that wants a table in every beat is a `visual-doc`. The table beat and the cost beat may share one group. A wide table scrolls inside its panel (`.fig` has `overflow-x:auto`); it never shrinks its type to fit.

### Evidence card, only when the drawing cannot say it

A drawing shows mechanism; it cannot show what a file actually says. An **evidence card** (`.ev`) between the figure and the notes quotes the real thing verbatim, in its own shape. The card is optional, and it earns its place in exactly three cases:

1. **Absence.** A line that should be in the file is not (a config key, a profile check, a field in a request), or a table lacks a column. The card shows the neighbors and a ghost line where the missing line belongs; when the source does not say where, put it where the same line sits in the path that has it (the first thing the function does, the line before the check it would have guarded).
2. **The wording is the change.** The claim is about what a text says: a prompt, an error message, a config value, a regex. Paraphrasing it would make the reader ask what it actually said. When the change is at one place in one file, the card is a **diff** (below), never two cards for before and after.
3. **The one line a box stands for.** The drawing abstracted a decision or a call into a box (`프로필 적용 헬퍼`), and the reader is better off seeing the one call or the one `if` than a sentence about it. This case is the strictest: at most one such card per beat, 8 lines or fewer not counting a gap line, and only when the box has a badge.

Not a card: code whose behavior the drawing already shows, a function signature, a file quoted to prove it was read, a second copy of something a card in another beat already quotes. If the notes could state the fact and the reader would believe them, there is no card. A page where every beat earns one may carry one on every beat; a page where none does carries none. `--code` relaxes case 3 to any badged box; `--no-code` removes every card and the notes carry the facts.

Six forms, one component:

- **A file** (YAML, config, a request body, a model class): `.ev-head` in the order a reader asks: **the file name or endpoint first** (`.loc`, in ink: it is what the card is about), then a kind word saying what view of it this is (`설정`, `있는 코드`, `없는 코드`, `요청`; `em` blue or `bad` red when it names the point or the break), then **the badge of the box this card backs** (`<span class="m code">`, the same number as in the drawing; the drawing's badge and the note's badge for that box take the `code` shape too). The frame pushes the badge to the right edge, where the eye lands when it comes down from the drawing looking for that number; write the three in that DOM order too. Then `.ev-body > pre` with one `.ln` per line, each `.ln` holding only `.t` (the text) and an optional `.tag`. Quote the lines as they are in the source, strip only the indentation common to all of them, and keep to about 12 lines (`… N줄 생략` for the rest). Lines carry no badge and no fill: this is a quote of the file as it is, not a change, and a red-filled line reads as a diff deletion. `.ln.hi` marks the line that is the point with a neutral wash and its `.tag` says why; `.ln.hi.bad` is the same wash with a red tag for the line that breaks. There is no gutter mark: a symbol whose meaning the reader has to guess (`›`) is not a signal. A `.tag` follows the text directly (`margin-left` only), never pushed to the right edge: a note pushed past the card's edge reads as cut off. A line that **should be there and is not** is a ghost line: `.ln.ghost`, the text the reader would expect in faint gray inside a gray dashed outline, a `.tag` saying `이 줄이 없음`; it gets no syntax color. Gray, not red: red says "this exists and is wrong", and the point of a ghost line is that nothing is there. Color the real lines with the shared syntax classes (`sx-k` keywords and booleans, `sx-f` names and YAML keys, `sx-s` strings, `sx-n` numbers, `sx-c` comments). A YAML file has keys, numbers, booleans and strings: it gets the colors too.
- **A diff** (the file form for a change at one place): every `.ln` starts with `<span class="sign">` holding `-`, `+`, or a space, then `.t`. Removed lines are `.ln.del` (red, minus), added lines `.ln.add` (green, plus), unchanged lines plain with a space sign; the gap line keeps the space sign. These are the same colors explain-diff uses for its diff lines, so a reader of both pages reads them the same way. The head reads the file, the kind `PR #n diff` (`add`), then the badges of both states when the drawing has both (two terminal badges at the right edge; the drawing shows which state is old with `.dg-dim`). The `-`/`+` signs and the red and green fills belong to this form only: a quote of unchanged code (the file form) has neither, or the reader takes it for a change. Keep the context to the lines that show where the change sits; a `.tag` on the removed line names what went (`최대 4단어`), one on the added line what came (`구별하는 말은 다 남긴다`). Never quote the old block in one card and the new block in another: the reader cannot see that they are the same place.
- **A table** (`.erd`): one `.tbl` per table, the real table name with a plain sub-line, one `.col` per column with the badge slot (`<span class="g">`, empty or holding the badge; a table card has no head badge because its badges sit on the columns) then `PK`/`FK` in the gutter and a short type or role on the right (`vector(1024) = 4 KB`, `→ vector_store_chunk.id`), the columns the beat uses in `.hi`, the rest listed and folded (`+ 7 컬럼`) rather than dropped. Two tables sit side by side with the `.rel` arrow between them; more than two is a `visual-doc`.
- **A request or response**: the file form with JSON, the fields of this case only, values the source gives (`…` for the rest), the missing field as a ghost line.
- **A prose source** (a system or user prompt, an error message, a notice: text written as sentences): the `.prose-card` in `frame.html`, never the `.ln` code form and never a box of your own. `.pc-h` holds the kind (`system`, `user`, `error`), the name (`RESTORE_SYS v3`) and the file at the right edge; `.pc-b` holds one `<p>` per source paragraph (mono face, 12.5px, line-height 1.75, 76ch wide, 12px between paragraphs). The words are verbatim and only whitespace may change: a list of conditions joined by `; or` may break at those joins (`<br>`), and a one-line JSON may be laid out one key per line in `<pre class="js">`. Nothing is added inside the card: no highlight, tag, fold or numbering; what the beat takes from the text is the note under it. A Korean translation is never a second card and never a gloss under the text: it is the other side of the `원문`/`번역` toggle of the prompt card (next item). Quote the paragraphs the beat uses and mark the rest with `<p class="gap">… N문단 생략</p>`; a card that needs more than about 40 lines means the text itself is the subject, which is a `visual-doc`.
- **A case with its fields** (one item of the data shown whole: what went in, what was expected, what each setting produced): named rows (`dl.rows` in a `.fig.tab` panel whose `.res-t` is the case's name), a name column and a text column, never a card of the page's own design. One row holds one thing, verbatim. When a field has several items in an order that matters (earlier turns, steps of a run), each item is its own row and the order is in the row name, in the subject's words (`이전 질문 1`, `이전 질문 2`; `1턴`, `2턴`), never a number set inside the text; a single item gets no number. A row is not colored: an outcome is one `.cap` pill at the end of its row (`cap em`, `cap bad`, plain), the pill a prompt card's result uses. The row the others are compared against (the expected value) is `dd.ref`, in the secondary color; a case that has no such value has no such row. A setting that produced nothing says so in the page's words, also as `dd.ref` (`고치지 않음`), with its pill. The pill is `bad` for an outcome that is a failure, `em` for the outcome the beat is about, plain otherwise, the same on every case of the page. The title is the case's own name or id as the source has it. A named-rows panel is not a table: it gets no number line and is not counted with the tables. A beat shows up to about three cases, stacked, under the beat's table or figure and above the notes.
- **A prompt with what went in and what came out** (the prompt card, `.pv` in `frame.html`): when the beat shows a prose source together with its input, the output it produced, a translation, or more than one version of it, these are one card. A run of separate prose cards with the same look (`v1`, its translation, its input, `v2`, …) leaves the reader to work out from the small head lines which card belongs to which. The head holds the name and, at its right edge, every toggle. The rows are the stages the source has, in order, each named in the left column: the prompt (`system`), the input (`user`, with the example's name), the output (with what produced it), and a result stage when the source has one (a verdict, a check). The prompt is verbatim text as in the prose form; input and output are verbatim in a gray inset (`<pre class="js">`); a result is one `.cap` pill in the color the page's figures use for that result (`cap em`, `cap bad`, plain) and nothing else: no reason beside it, no number said again. **Versions**: the default is one card with a version toggle. The side-by-side form (`.pv.cmp`: one column per version, a gray strip naming each stage) is used only when the user asks to see the versions together. **Translation**: the original shows first; the `원문`/`번역` toggle swaps the prompt for its translation, set in the body face, and exists only when a translation does. **Toggles** (`.tg`) all sit at the right edge of the head, those that choose what is shown (version, model) first and the one that changes how it is shown (language) last. A toggle holds up to three choices; from four it becomes the dropdown (`.pop`: the selected piece with a chevron, a menu in the same face). Every block a toggle switches carries `data-when` (`ver=v5`, `lang=ko`, or both), and the page then includes the `prompt card` script from the end of `frame.html`. A stage the source has for only some versions carries `data-when` on its whole `.pv-row`; a stage the source gives in another shape for one version (a bare string where the other is JSON) is shown as the source has it, never wrapped to match. The card opens on the version the beat's closing line is about, usually the newest. The card sits where evidence cards sit, between the figure and the notes; it has no badge and is tied to the drawing by its name, so the box it backs carries the same name. These toggles switch between quoted sources, not states of a figure, so "An interactive figure" does not govern them; what a figure compares is still drawn statically.

A card never carries a sentence; what the reader should take from it is the note with the same badge.

Cards stack one per row (`.evs`). Two cards go side by side (`.evs.pair`) only when they pass "Rows by default" in the design rules: short lines in both, and the reader reads them together.

### A list of many items, only when the reader pages through them

- **When.** The source has 9 or more items of the same shape that the reader looks through one by one (the questions of a test set, the cases of a review, the rows a pipeline kept and dropped). Eight or fewer are a results table or a figure. Like the results table, the list is a response to the shape of the source: most pages have none, and a page has one at most.
- **How.** Only then, read `list.html` from this skill's base directory and build the list from it. Its style, markup and script are copied verbatim; you fill the caption, the column names, the three column widths, `LM_CFG` and the rows. Its header comment holds the rules (one caption line and no text under the list, nothing under a badge, tabs for the first split, fixed short columns and one long-text column, named lines in the long-text cell, color only for what failed, 8 rows a page): follow them from the file, do not restate them from memory, and do not build a table of your own for this. A list beat is a heading and the list: no notes, no closing line, no legend. It is a section of its own, after the beats that explain what its rows are.
- **Review column.** The list can carry a review column: two marks per row, kept in the browser as a draft, with a copy button (and a send button where the page is published as an artifact with the `db` and `user` capabilities; `list.html` rule 9). Turn it on (`LM_CFG.review`) only when the user said they will judge the rows themselves. If they did not say so and the rows look like something a person signs off one by one (a set to be confirmed, cases to accept or reject), ask before building whether to add it. Otherwise leave it off.

## Step 4: Build the Page

Read `frame.html` from this skill's base directory. It carries the locked design tokens (Upstage Lift: a white ground, zinc-leaning neutrals, brand `#5B52FF`, the lime highlight `#D2FF95`, both themes, plus aliases for the pre-Lift token names), the page frame, the cover (`.band` holding `.hero` and the nested `.outline`), the beat primitives (`.beat`, `.fig`, `.notes`/`.note` and `.notes.plain`, `.say` with `.lab`, `.close`), the `.m`/`.dg-m` badges, the `.dg-grp` group outline, the `.dg-bar` bars, the optional `.ev` evidence card (the badge in its head, diff lines), the `.pv` prompt card with its toggles and `.ctl` controls for an interactive figure, the section hierarchy block, and the SVG label classes with worked example drawings. **Copy the tokens and primitives verbatim, author the drawings bespoke.** Every page has different pictures; that is the whole point.

**Where the brand color goes.** The brand tint is a surface in exactly one place, the cover band; the figure panels are neutral. Brand purple marks the section numbers, the one `em` element of a drawing, the code chip, the cover's row names and the `정리` label; nothing else. The lime highlight appears only as the `.lab` of a closing line. Red is reserved for what breaks. Everything else is ink on white with hairlines: the body has two kinds of box (the figure panel and the evidence card) and no other card, tile, callout or colored band. A page that adds a third kind of box, a colored heading, a shadow or a gradient has left the system.

**One inset, one gap.** Every panel (a figure, a table, a prose card, a prompt card) has the same inner padding, 16px above and below and 20px at the sides, and the blocks of a beat (figure, card, notes, fold, closing line) are 16px apart in whatever order they come. Both come from `frame.html`. Never put a margin or a padding on a block to adjust it, and never build a new kind of panel with its own inset: a block that needs one is a sign the page wants a component the frame does not have, which goes in the Step 6 report.

**Where the reader is on the page.** Three marks, all inside the column: the 3px `.progress` bar right after the theme button fills with the scroll ratio and carries no text; the `.outline` inside the cover band lists every top-level section once as a hairline-separated row, each line the same text as its h2, with a grouped section's beats as a nested `<ol>` under its title (same 36px number column, so titles align on two vertical lines; no label, no card); and every top-level `<section>` carries `sec-top`, an `id`, and a `.sec-no` line before the h2 (`1 / 4`: this section's number and the section count, plain digits, never zero-padded). The h2 text never carries the number. The `.close` block has no heading and stays out of the outline. Nothing floats beside or above the column: no side rail, no sticky header with the section name. (explain-diff, micro-world and visual-doc still carry the pre-Lift version of this block; porting is a separate revision.)

**Group the beats when the page has a shape.** A page of up to four beats is one flat list. When there are six or more beats (or five with parts the reader would clearly name) and they fall into two to four parts (what the thing is, where it broke, the two changes, what it costs), the parts become the top-level sections and the beats go inside them: the part is a `<section class="sec-top" id="g1">` with the `.sec-no` line (`1 / 4`: part number and part count) and an `h2` stating the part's fact; each beat inside is a `.beat` with an `id` that opens with `.sub-h` (a `.sub-no` line `1-1`, part and beat joined by a hyphen, never `01.1`, and an `h3` with the beat's short heading; nothing under the h3) instead of its own rule and h2. A part with one beat is `sec-top single` and has no `.sub-h`: the part is the beat. The outline then lists the parts as top-level lines and the beats as a nested `<ol>` under each part (see `frame.html`), each the same text as its heading. Grouping is decided in the storyboard (Step 2), in the page's language, and a part's heading passes the same tests as a beat's. Never group to look organized: a flat page whose beats are one story reads better flat.

The type scale is part of what is locked: title 26-32px, section heading 21px, beat heading 17.5px, the numbers beside them (outline 15px and 14px for a nested row, the section number line 16px, a beat's number 14.5px), the cover's fact rows 16px with 13px row names, lede 16px, `.say` and notes 14.5px regular in the secondary text color (bold or the cost color on one phrase at most), the `정리` sentence 19px. Do not enlarge anything to make it "pop", and do not give `.say` a `max-width`; it runs the full column width, the same as the figure panel. The page ends at the `.close` block: a hairline, the mono `정리` label, one bold sentence, one short paragraph, no box and no background.

Delete the authoring comment at the top of `frame.html` and every placeholder token (every Korean phrase in braces, such as `{출처 한 가지, YYYY-MM-DD}` and `{이 그림이 보여주는 사실 한 가지. 개조식 가능}`, plus the sample beat copy) as you fill each block. A shipped page must contain none of them.

Output is a single self-contained HTML file. Fill `<title>`: a short noun phrase naming the subject, not a summary.

### Design rules (shared across eli5, explain-diff, micro-world, visual-doc)

- **No redundant or meta text; this rule comes first.** (shared across eli5, explain-diff, micro-world, visual-doc) Every line on the page says something the reader does not already have. A heading, a chip, a row caption or a card head is said once: no sub-line, tag or caption that restates it in other words (`현재: 모노 글꼴 영어 벽` beside a block that visibly is one, `결과 문서의 블록을 그대로 옮김`). The page never describes its own form: no words about fonts, sizes, units, emphasis, folding, columns, or what a block "shows" (`본문 글꼴`, `문단 단위`, `해설이 가리키는 문단만 강조`, `표로 정리`), and it never names the skill, the template or the process that made it. A deck states a fact about the subject, never what the section is about (`이 절에서는`). When a sentence would be true of the page rather than of the subject, delete it. Render check: read every sub-line, tag, deck and caption aloud and strike the ones that describe the page.
- **Voice: prose in 해요체, as to a colleague; 개조식 only where the heading rules apply.** (shared across eli5, explain-diff, micro-world, visual-doc) Korean running text (lede, notes, closing lines, takeaways, prose paragraphs, sentence-shaped tooltips) is in 해요체, the register of explaining something to a colleague who asked: `검색에는 쓰이지 않아요`, `원문 그대로 둬요`. Not the childish end of 해요체 (`만들어요`, `안 가요`, `하나도 안 나빠졌어요` in a technical sentence) and not 한다체 or 합니다체. A sentence ends in a predicate: a noun or a stand-in noun at the end (`~하는 꼴. 20건.`, `~한 것.`) is telegraphese and is rewritten as a sentence (`~하는 질문이에요. 20건.`). Every noun the sentence leans on is concrete: `빠진 이름` becomes `이전 질문에만 있던 문서 이름이나 판`. 개조식 (nominal fragments) belongs to headings, decks, chips, SVG labels and sub-lines, table cells and the outline. The length caps on notes do not change the register: a shorter note has fewer facts, not clipped endings. Render check: the Voice count below.
- **Title never wraps mid-word.** `word-break: keep-all` stays on titles and narrative text so a Korean particle (`로`, `를`, `이`) can never fall to the start of a line. Titles are short noun phrases that state a fact about the subject (see "제목은 그림이 무엇인지 말한다").
- **No decorative gradients.** Backgrounds are solid tokens. A gradient is allowed only when functional (a fade scrim), never as panel decoration.
- **No machine-writing symbols anywhere in the output.** (shared across eli5, explain-diff, micro-world, visual-doc) No em-dash (`—`), en-dash (`–`), or middle dot (`·`) in titles, prose, captions, labels, SVG text, TOC descriptions, hints, quiz text, eyebrows, or code-block headers. They are machine-writing tells. A dash becomes a colon, parentheses, a comma, or two sentences: write `옵션 1만 vs 옵션 1+2: 권장안과 근거`, never the same with a dash. A middle dot between items becomes a comma or `와`/`과` (`설치와 로그인`, `제목, 소제목, 라벨`); a dot used as a separator in a label becomes a comma or a line break. A range is `~` (`1~3`). The one exception is source quoted verbatim in a code block or an evidence card, which keeps its characters exactly; in a prose quote (`.quote2`, an inline quote) quote a shorter span that does not contain one, and never rewrite a quote to remove it. Before finishing, `grep -nP '[—–·]' <file>` must print nothing except lines of such verbatim source.
- **Color must survive its background.** A mark carrying meaning (a legend swatch, a status dot, an emphasized stroke) must contrast with the surface under it in both themes.
- **Rows by default; side by side only when the render proves it fits.** (shared across eli5, explain-diff, micro-world, visual-doc) When two things are shown for comparison (two states, two options, two code blocks, a request and its response, two evidence cards), stack them as rows: each keeps the full column width and the eye reads down instead of jumping across. Side by side is a judgment call, not a ban, and it is judged on the rendered page, not by counting characters: it stays only when nothing inside either half scrolls sideways and the reader gains from seeing the two at the same height (two short lists, two table schemas, a control and the view it changes). To measure, append `measure.html` from this skill's base directory to a copy of the page and read the result with the one-line command in its header comment: `data-sbs` names every multi-column grid with something scrolling sideways inside it, and every block it names is stacked. Without a browser there is no measurement, so stack.
- **Both light and dark, switchable.** Never hardcode a color that breaks one mode. The page follows the system setting by default, and the fixed `.theme-btn` in the top-right corner lets the reader override it. Copy three things verbatim and together: the button markup right after `<body>`, the `.theme-btn` CSS, and the small theme `<script>` in `<head>`. The dark tokens are defined twice on purpose (`@media (prefers-color-scheme: dark)` on `:root:not([data-theme="light"])`, and `:root[data-theme="dark"]`); keep the two blocks identical and never drop one, or the toggle stops winning in one direction. Before finishing, confirm all three parts of the toggle survived: `grep -c '<button type="button" class="theme-btn"'`, `grep -c '\.theme-btn{'`, and `grep -c 'git-claw-doc-theme'` must each print at least 1 (a bare `theme-btn` match can be prose that merely mentions it).
- **Code blocks: one shared component.** Show code only inside the template's `.codeblock` (`.cb-head` with the location on the left (`파일, 함수`) and the language on the right, then `.cb-body > pre`, one `.ln` span per line). Copy its CSS block verbatim; it is identical in all four templates. Strip the indentation common to every quoted line and keep the relative indentation; nothing else in the text changes. Replace skipped lines with one `<span class="ln gap">… N줄 생략</span>` (`… 생략` when the source does not give the count; never invent N), indented to the depth of the code it replaces (in an evidence card the gap line is `<span class="ln gap"><span class="t">… N줄 생략</span></span>` and in a diff card keeps the space `.sign`). Color syntax by wrapping tokens yourself, never with a highlighter script or CDN: `sx-k` keywords (`if`, `and`, `return`, `function`), `sx-f` function names where called or defined, `sx-s` string literals, `sx-n` numbers and language constants (`True`, `None`, `null`), `sx-c` comments. Leave variables, operators, and punctuation unwrapped, and HTML-escape `<`, `>`, `&`. Never hard-wrap a long line or set `pre-wrap`: the block scrolls sideways with an always-visible scrollbar. On this page there is no standalone `.codeblock`: quoted source lines go in the file form of the evidence card (`.ev`, see "Evidence card" above), which reuses only the `sx-*` classes and the strip-indent and `… N줄 생략` rules from this item, and appears only when the drawing cannot say it.
- **Natural Korean, not machine translation.** Do not coin stiff 한자어 Koreans do not say; keep a code identifier verbatim rather than force-translate it. Read every heading and `.say` aloud once: if it sounds like a translated subtitle, rewrite it plainer.
- **No metaphor, no implied meaning, no translationese in titles and labels.** A heading, a chip, a row caption, a sub-line, a tooltip states the fact in the subject's own words (`vector_store_file 1행`, `호출 최대 N회`, `원본 테이블`), terse 개조식 welcome. Not `장부`, `선반`, `고리를 돈다`, `~의 여정`, `한눈에 보는 ~`, `숨은 비용`, `마법은 없다`. A phrase that would sound odd said aloud to a colleague is cut; a phrase that could head a page about a different subject is too abstract.
- **Headings are answers, not frames; plan them in the output language.** (shared across eli5, explain-diff, micro-world, visual-doc) Write the section plan in the language the page will be in, with the final heading text, before any HTML: a heading drafted in English and translated comes out as `시험을 어떻게 내는가` or `점수 읽는 법`. The patterns below are the tells, and each is replaced by the fact it was hiding. A question (`~하는가`, `~인가`, `~일까`, `왜 ~인가`) becomes its answer: `점수를 어떻게 읽는가` is `점수 세 종류: pass@1, 승률, 시간 지평`. A classification noun (`N축`, `~한 축`, `차원`, `관점`, `레이어`, `측면`) becomes the names of the things: `평가의 4축` is `채점 방식 3가지: 테스트, 루브릭, 사람 비교`. A method noun (`~하는 법`, `~읽는 법`, `~하려면`) becomes the subject and its values. A bare `A vs B` becomes the value that differs: `pass@1 vs pass^k` is `pass@1은 1회 성공, pass^k는 k회 연속 성공`. An emphasis frame (`핵심은`, `결국`, `본질적으로`, `진짜`, `단순히 ~가 아니다`, `~에 불과`) is deleted. A count is counted after the items exist, never chosen first and padded (`세 가지`). A metaphor that frames the whole subject (`모의고사`, `시험지`, `장부`) stays out of every heading and out of the plan. None of these words is banned in body prose, where `A vs B` or `~하려면` can be the plain sentence: the rule is about headings, captions, outline lines, and the plan, which is why the Render check's heading grep reads only heading tags and reports a reading list, not failures.
